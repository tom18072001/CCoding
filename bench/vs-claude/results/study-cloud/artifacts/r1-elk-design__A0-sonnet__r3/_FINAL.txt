I wrote `DESIGN.md` at the repo root. I haven't committed it, and none of the sizing has been tested against real events.

**Sizing**
- **Raw volume:** 1.728 billion events a day, which is 1.38 TB/day or 16 MB/s. A year is 504.6 TB.
- **Hot tier (days 0–7):** 6 nodes with 4 TB NVMe each. It holds 13.5 TB of data including one replica, and needs about 19.4 TB of disk at 70 % full.
- **Warm tier (days 8–30):** 6 nodes with 8 TB each. It holds 31.8 TB of data including one replica, and needs about 45.4 TB of disk at 70 % full.
- **Restore tier:** 4 on-demand nodes with 8 TB each, for data older than 30 days.
- **Object storage:** about 387 TB for 400 days. That is roughly 111 TB of signed evidence archive plus about 276 TB of snapshots.
- **Compression:** I assumed index size is 0.7× raw on hot and 0.5× raw after merging. If real data compresses worse, disk needs rise by roughly 45 %. The document says to pilot with 1–2 days of real events before buying hardware.

**Conflicts in the brief**
- **Searchable snapshots:** the customer's suggestion cannot work, because they need an Enterprise license and the budget is Basic. The design uses ordinary snapshots plus a restore tier instead. A restore of 30 days takes about 11.5 hours, so the 24-hour requirement holds for requests up to about 30 days of data.
- **Masking vs "provably unaltered":** masking changes the original event, so what the customer can prove unaltered is the masked record. Legal needs to decide whether that is acceptable.
- **Unmasked data in the buffer:** raw events sit unmasked in the Kafka buffer for up to 48 hours before masking.
- **Basic license gaps:** Basic has no native audit log, no document- or field-level security, and no LDAP or SAML. The design compensates with proxy and OS logging.

Integrity does not rely on Elasticsearch. Each event gets a SHA-256 hash, and signed, timestamped, hash-chained batch manifests go to write-once (S3 Object Lock) storage. A daily job re-verifies the chain against what Elasticsearch holds. The snapshot copy itself cannot sit under Object Lock, so it is checked against the archive on restore.

The document also covers the node layout, the ingest pipeline with masking rules, the ILM and snapshot-policy JSON, and the restore procedure. It ends with the risk register and what changes if the customer buys Enterprise.

I wrote the license claims and some ILM options from memory, so verify them before this goes to the customer. The license claims are the Basic vs Enterprise/Gold feature split. The ILM option is `index_codec` on force-merge, which only exists in newer 8.x/9.x. Also check the restore throughput figure (about 500 MB/s across 4 nodes).
